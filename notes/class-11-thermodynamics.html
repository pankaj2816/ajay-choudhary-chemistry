Class 11 · Chapter 5

Chemical Thermodynamics

Energy changes in reactions, enthalpy, entropy and Gibbs energy, and how to tell whether a reaction is spontaneous.

2 min read

The big idea

Thermodynamics answers one question: will this change happen on its own? Two tendencies decide it: systems like to lose energy (ΔH negative) and to spread out (ΔS positive). Gibbs energy combines both.

Words you must know

  • System: the part you study. Surroundings: everything else.
  • Open / closed / isolated: exchanges matter and energy / only energy / nothing.
  • State function: depends only on the start and end states, not the path (U, H, S, G). Path functions: q (heat) and w (work).

First law: energy is conserved

ΔU = q + w

  • q is positive when heat goes into the system; w is positive when work is done on the system.
  • Work of expansion against constant pressure: w = −p_ext ΔV.

Enthalpy (H)

ΔH = ΔU + Δn_g RT, where Δn_g = moles of gaseous products − moles of gaseous reactants.

  • ΔH < 0: exothermic (heat released). ΔH > 0: endothermic.
  • Hess's law: the total ΔH is the same whether a reaction happens in one step or many, so you can add and subtract equations like algebra.
  • Bond enthalpy method: ΔH = Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed).

Entropy and Gibbs energy

  • Entropy (S) measures disorder. Gas > liquid > solid. Dissolving, melting and making more gas molecules increase S.
  • Gibbs energy: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
  • ΔG < 0 → spontaneous. ΔG > 0 → non-spontaneous. ΔG = 0 → equilibrium.
  • ΔG° = −RT ln K links thermodynamics to equilibrium.

Tip: "Spontaneous" means possible, not fast. Diamond turning into graphite is spontaneous but takes millions of years.

Common mistakes

  • Using °C instead of kelvin in ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
  • Mixing units: ΔH is usually in kJ, ΔS in J/K. Convert one before combining.
  • Sign of work: the system doing work on the surroundings means w is negative.

Quick revision

  • Heat at constant volume = ΔU; heat at constant pressure = ΔH.
  • Standard enthalpy of formation of an element in its standard state = 0.
  • Cp − Cv = R for an ideal gas.
