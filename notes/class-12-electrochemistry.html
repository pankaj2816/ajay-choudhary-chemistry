Class 12 · Chapter 2

Electrochemistry

Galvanic cells, electrode potentials, the Nernst equation, conductance and electrolysis.

2 min read

The big idea

A redox reaction moves electrons. If you separate the oxidation and reduction and connect them with a wire, those electrons flow through the wire as electric current. That is a battery.

Galvanic (electrochemical) cell

  • Anode: oxidation, negative terminal. Cathode: reduction, positive terminal. (Memory: An Ox, Red Cat.)
  • Salt bridge completes the circuit and keeps both solutions neutral.
  • Daniell cell: Zn | Zn²⁺ || Cu²⁺ | Cu, E°cell = 1.10 V.

Electrode potential

  • Standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) is the reference: E° = 0.00 V.
  • E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode (use reduction potentials for both).
  • A higher (more positive) reduction potential means a stronger oxidising agent.
  • E°cell > 0 → the cell reaction is spontaneous.

Nernst equation

E_cell = E°cell − (0.0591 / n) log Q (at 298 K)

  • At equilibrium E_cell = 0, so E°cell = (0.0591 / n) log K.
  • ΔG° = −nFE°cell (F = 96 500 C mol⁻¹).

Conductance

  • Conductivity (κ) decreases on dilution (fewer ions per unit volume).
  • Molar conductivity (Λm) increases on dilution.
  • Strong electrolytes: Λm rises slightly, Λm = Λ°m − A√C. Weak electrolytes: Λm rises sharply on dilution.
  • Kohlrausch's law: Λ°m = sum of the limiting molar conductivities of the ions. Used to find Λ°m of weak electrolytes and their degree of dissociation (α = Λm / Λ°m).

Electrolysis and Faraday's laws

  • Mass deposited m = (M / nF) × I × t. Charge Q = I × t.
  • Products depend on electrode potentials and overpotential; aqueous NaCl gives Cl₂ at the anode and H₂ at the cathode, not Na.
Tip: Rusting is an electrochemical cell: iron acts as the anode where it is oxidised, and oxygen is reduced at another spot on the surface.

Quick revision

  • Dry cell and lead storage battery: the lead battery is rechargeable (secondary).
  • H₂–O₂ fuel cell product is water; used in spacecraft.
  • Galvanising (zinc coating) protects iron because zinc is oxidised more easily.
